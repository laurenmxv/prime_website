Lake Nona Landscaping Considerations

As a master-planned community still actively under development, Lake Nona presents a distinct set of landscaping needs compared to older Central Florida neighborhoods.

Much of Lake Nona sits on soils that were former agricultural or wetland land — sandy, fast-draining, and low in organic matter. New construction sites in particular often have soil that's been graded, compacted, and stripped of topsoil, which makes establishing turf and plant material a challenge in the first few years. We account for this with proper soil prep, starter fertilization programs, and appropriate irrigation scheduling during the establishment phase.

Because so much of the community is new construction, irrigation is a major service need. Builders often install basic systems that need to be properly programmed, zoned, and adjusted after residents move in. We handle new system installs for custom homes, irrigation audits and repairs on builder-installed systems, and smart controller upgrades that tie into Florida-friendly watering schedules.

HOA requirements in Laureate Park and Eagle Creek are detailed and actively enforced. These communities were designed with a cohesive streetscape in mind, and landscaping that deviates from established standards can result in notices. We're familiar with these requirements and work with property managers and homeowners to keep compliance issues from becoming recurring problems.

The commercial district along Lake Nona Boulevard — including the USTA National Campus, medical facilities, and the Town Center retail area — requires grounds maintenance that fits a professional campus environment. Manicured turf, clean bed edges, and consistent appearance are non-negotiable for these high-profile properties.

Why Lake Nona properties choose us

What makes a good Lake Nona landscaper different.

Lake Nona isn't like the rest of Orlando. Master-planned communities, medical campuses, and brand-new construction all need a landscaper who works to a higher standard — not just one who shows up with a mower.

A landscaping company built for master-planned communities

Most landscaping companies in Central Florida grew up servicing older Orlando neighborhoods — uneven lots, established trees, decades-old turf, and HOAs that range from inactive to barely enforced. Lake Nona is the opposite. Laureate Park, Eagle Creek, Village Walk, and the Lake Nona Town Center area were all designed at once, to a cohesive standard, with covenants that are enforced weekly. A landscaper who delivers a "good enough" job in an older subdivision will draw violation notices in Lake Nona within a month.

We specifically built our crew, schedule, and reporting system for this kind of community. Same crew every visit means the people on your property already know which beds get cypress mulch versus pine straw, what mowing height the HOA expects, and where the irrigation valves are located. We deliver photo-documented service reports after every visit — board-ready documentation if you're on an HOA committee, peace of mind if you're a homeowner.

Custom design and installation in Lake Nona

When residents search for Lake Nona custom landscapers, they're usually solving one of three problems: (1) the builder's "package" landscape was minimal and the home needs proper plantings, (2) the existing landscape is mature but doesn't match the home's aesthetic, or (3) they want hardscape — paver walkways, fire features, paver expansions — that the original build didn't include.

We handle all three. Our custom installation process starts with a site walk where we measure existing plantings, identify irrigation zones, and discuss the look you want. We design around Lake Nona's sandy soil and Zone 9b climate — drought-tolerant where it makes sense, formal where the architecture demands it. Floratam St. Augustine is the most common turf choice for Lake Nona's full-sun lots; for shaded areas around mature oaks, Palmetto St. Augustine performs better. For commercial properties along the Lake Nona Boulevard corridor we usually recommend Zoysia for its tighter blade and lower water requirement.

Working with Lake Nona's HOAs and builders

Both Tavistock-developed communities and the smaller HOAs around the Medical City have specific approval processes for landscape changes. Any plantings outside the home's lot boundary, any hardscape additions, and most tree removals require ARC (Architectural Review Committee) approval. We submit ARC packets on your behalf when needed — including before/after renderings, plant list with species and quantities, and any irrigation modifications. The result: fewer board notices, faster approvals, and projects that don't get held up at the property-manager level.

For new builds in Lake Nona's expansion zones — Storey Park, Beacon Park, and the newer Pulte and Lennar sections — we work directly with builders' preferred-vendor lists and can handle the post-close landscape installation that builders typically defer to the homeowner. Often we can install before move-in, so you arrive at a fully landscaped home rather than a graded sand pad.
